# Insurance and health abroad
## Penn requirements [FACT] (global.upenn.edu/pennabroad/health-insurance; global.upenn.edu/gss/travel-guidance/insurance-and-24-7-assistance-abroad — checked 2026-11-10)
- Keep medical insurance valid in your home country while abroad. Students on the Penn Student Insurance Plan must stay enrolled.
- Students on Penn-affiliated international travel are **International SOS (ISOS)** members: travel medical insurance, 24/7
  medical & security advice, hospital referrals, evacuation assistance.
- To be pre-authorised for ISOS medical insurance you must **register your round-trip flight itinerary in MyTrips**.
- Contact **ISOS first** before seeking non-emergency care abroad (helps with payment). Penn travel safety: travelsafety@pobox.upenn.edu.
## UK healthcare
- [FACT] Emergency: **999**; urgent non-emergency advice: **111** (nhs.uk). Police non-emergency: 101.
- [UNVERIFIED] Visitors (ETA, under 6 months) can usually register with and see an NHS GP free, but hospital treatment is
  generally **chargeable** to overseas visitors who haven't paid the immigration health surcharge; A&E emergency care is free at
  the point of use, admissions are not. Sources found were NHS trust pages from 2013–2023 — confirm on gov.uk / nhs.uk
  "NHS entitlements: migrant health guide" before relying on it. ISOS is the main route for paid care.
- Host-school services: QMUL Advice & Counselling, Disability & Dyslexia Service, Student Health (see institutions/*).
## Bringing medicine [FACT] (gov.uk/take-medicine-in-or-out-uk, checked 2026-11-10)
- Carry a prescription or doctor's letter proving the medicine is yours.
- Controlled drugs: more than 3 months' supply needs a personal licence (Home Office Drug & Firearms Licensing Unit);
  Schedule 1 drugs need a licence regardless. Check your medicine against the UK controlled-drugs list before travel —
  some common US ADHD medicines are controlled in the UK.
